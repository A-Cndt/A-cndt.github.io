Night Sky of the Month: March 2025

Monthly sky guide: constellations, planets, and astronomical events.

March brings a transition sky with bright constellations and major astronomical events.

Constellations of the Month

Leo (the Lion)

Visibility: All night

Description: Leo is one of the most recognizable spring constellations, highlighted by its bright star Regulus.

Tips: Look for the backward question mark shape to locate the lion’s head.

  • Regulus: brightest star in Leo
  • Leo Triplet: M65, M66, and NGC 3628 (Hamburger Galaxy)

Virgo (the Maiden)

Visibility: After midnight

Description: Virgo is a large constellation containing the bright star Spica.

Tips: Follow the arc from the Big Dipper to locate Spica.

  • Spica: brightest star in Virgo
  • Virgo Cluster: large galaxy cluster
  • Sombrero Galaxy: iconic edge-on spiral galaxy

Visible Planets in March

Jupiter

Visibility: Early night

Description: Extremely bright planet visible after sunset.

Tips: Look toward the western horizon after sunset.

Saturn

Visibility: Before dawn

Description: Located in Aquarius during March.

Tips: Observe in a clear eastern sky before sunrise.

Mars

Visibility: Midnight

Description: Red planet visible in Gemini.

Tips: Best observed under dark skies.

Venus

Visibility: After sunset

Description: Extremely bright evening object.

Tips: Visible shortly after sunset in the west.

Astronomical Events of March

Partial Lunar Eclipse

Date: March 14, 2025

Description: Part of the Moon passes through Earth’s shadow, visible from Europe.

Tips: Observe early morning before sunrise.

Spring Equinox

Date: March 20, 2025

Description: Day and night are equal in length, marking the beginning of spring in the Northern Hemisphere.

Daylight Saving Time

Date: Night of March 29–30, 2025

Description: Clocks move forward by one hour at 2:00 AM.

Partial Solar Eclipse

Date: March 29, 2025

Description: Visible in parts of Europe, Africa, and Asia.

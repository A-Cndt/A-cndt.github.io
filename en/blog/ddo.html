The role of the Launch Operations Director (DDO) at the Guiana Space Centre: a key role for European launchers

Published on March 18, 2025 by Alexandre Condette

"To all DDO, attention for the final countdown", this is the iconic phrase pronounced by the DDO (Launch Operations Director) during every rocket launch from the Guiana Space Centre (CSG) in Kourou.

The Guiana Space Centre, located in Kourou, is one of the most strategic launch sites in the world. From this base, Ariane and Vega rockets are launched. At the heart of this operational infrastructure lies a key position: the Launch Operations Director.

This role, often unknown to the general public, is nevertheless essential for the success of launch missions. With the arrival of Ariane 6, the DDO role becomes even more critical.

Who is the DDO, what is their role?

A conductor of launch operations

The Launch Operations Director supervises and coordinates all critical phases of a space launch. They ensure synchronization between technical teams, ground operators, and mission stakeholders.

They organize every stage of the launch, from system preparation to orbital insertion, following a strict timeline and highly rigorous procedures.

Technical and organizational expertise

The DDO has advanced expertise in launch systems, telemetry, and safety protocols. They analyze real-time data throughout the critical phases.

In case of a technical, environmental, or safety anomaly, they must act immediately to secure the mission.

A decisive role in the countdown

The DDO is at the center of the countdown process, a highly structured and strictly controlled sequence.

Phrases such as "Attention for H0" or "Attention for the final countdown" are part of their official communications.

Decision-making under extreme pressure

In case of technical or weather anomalies, the DDO must react immediately and make critical decisions.

They can decide to abort or continue the launch within seconds.

Their composure and analytical ability are essential to mission success.

DDO responsibilities on Ariane 6

A more flexible launcher

Ariane 6 represents a major evolution of European launch systems and significantly changes operations.

The DDO must now manage:

  • More flexible launcher: A62 and A64 configurations adapted to mission needs.
  • Increased launch cadence: higher frequency of launches.
  • Enhanced automation: more complex control systems.

Managing automated systems

Automation improves precision but increases operational complexity.

The DDO must supervise automated systems while maintaining maximum safety.

They must also anticipate risks and ensure coordination between technical teams.

Launch day: extreme pressure

Continuous monitoring

From the Jupiter control room, the DDO supervises every second of the launch.

Global coordination

All services must confirm nominal parameters before each critical step.

Final decision

The DDO can abort the countdown up to the very last second.

Critical role in case of anomaly

In case of an incident, the DDO must react immediately and activate emergency procedures.

They analyze the situation in real time and take the necessary decisions to ensure safety.

They are the final safeguard before launch.

A demanding and prestigious career

Experience-based path

Becoming a DDO requires strong experience in space engineering and launch operations.

Most DDOs come from CNES, ESA, or the aerospace industry.

A strategic evolving role

With Ariane 6 and the rise of private space companies, the role is becoming increasingly strategic.

The DDO is a key actor in mission planning, safety, and success.
